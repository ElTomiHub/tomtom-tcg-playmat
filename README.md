# Pokémon TCG Playmat

Keep `index.html`, `playmat.css`, `logo.png`, `LimitlessBridge.ps1`, `StartLocalApp.bat`, and the `assets` folder together. Double-click **StartLocalApp.bat** to open `index.html` from its local file path and start the card lookup bridge. Keeping the app at the same file path preserves its existing browser-saved decks and cards. Keep the PowerShell window open while using Limitless lookup; press **Ctrl+C** in that window to stop the bridge. The bridge binds to `127.0.0.1` only and serves a small set of local files; it does not publish the site online.

Card lookup and artwork need an internet connection. The local bridge reads exact English Limitless card pages and returns card details to the browser. If an exact print lookup fails, the app retries through Limitless name search and then TCGdex; collector numbers with leading zeroes are treated as the same number (for example, `67` and `067`). The **Retry missing data** button rechecks all saved cards that lack verified setup details and shows how many succeeded or still failed. **Copy unchecked IDs** copies the name, print ID, category hint, and lookup error so an unresolved card can be identified separately and its verified effect text added later. Opening `index.html` directly still works, but Limitless lookup requires the local bridge; use the launcher for the full experience. Card and deck data are saved in this browser's local storage.

For static hosting such as GitHub Pages, publish `index.html`, `playmat.css`, `logo.png`, and the `assets` folder together.

## Main menu

- **Start a game**: choose registered decks for both players. Decks need a valid 60-card list, a verified Basic Pokémon, and verified card details (card type, and Pokémon stage, HP, and type). Cards with unsupported effects do not block the game; a warning lists them before the coin flip. You can continue with supported actions, but unsupported effects remain blocked without consuming cards or turn allowances.

The matchup screen places Toma’s deck on the left and Yami’s on the right, with their covers and compositions. Each panel shows that player’s starts, wins and losses, completed games, and the deck’s overall record. The central rivalry panel shows completed results for the selected decks in those player seats and across all seats. Earlier matches without saved opponent information are excluded from matchup totals. Click the central coin to verify the decks and flip; click again to reflip, then choose **Play match** to deal. Changing a deck clears the coin decision. Ready deck names have no extra status label; missing data and unsupported effects keep their warnings.

- **Manage card library**: search Limitless or import cards from a deck. The saved record includes the exact print, image, card stats, attacks, abilities, rules, and card text returned by the lookup. Effects remain **Registered** until the local engine has a matching handler. Select a card to update, check, or delete it; use **Retry missing data** to look up unchecked cards in a batch. Deleting a card warns if saved decks still reference it.
- **Update all cards** checks saved card text against the current engine registry and shows a progress bar. It updates status labels; it does not generate new engine code or refresh every card's online data. Basic Energy is treated as implemented because it has no additional effect.
- **Copy pending cards** copies verified card text for cards marked **Registered**. Paste it into Codex to request engine handlers; then reopen the app and click **Update all cards** to recalculate their statuses.
- **Manage decks**: review deck status, composition, play results, win rate, the most-used non-Energy card, Pokémon Knock Outs and defeats, and opening-hand probabilities. The comparison table separates Toma, Yami, and Total. Play, history, cover, and edit controls sit beside each deck’s name. Expand **Deck rankings by win rate** to compare decks. You can start a deck with manual-effect warnings, or update its card data from the library. New decks can be saved by pasting a Limitless deck export; the deck and its card records are saved together even when some prints still need data. Edit updates the existing deck in place. Choose a card from the deck or upload a local cover image.

Use **Change cover** beside any saved deck to open its cover controls directly. Choose a card or upload a picture, then **Save changes**. Selecting a card replaces an uploaded cover picture.

The deck manager and card library fill the app window. Their collections scroll independently on desktop, with scrolling available in compact layouts and narrow comparison tables. Excluding Energy from the Most used highlight changes only the display; saved usage statistics and deck composition counts are preserved.

Card implementation status means:

- **Implemented**: every saved effect has an engine handler.
- **Registered**: card details are saved, but at least one effect still needs code. The game can continue after the warning, but unsupported actions are blocked.
- **Not checked**: the app could not verify enough card details to set up the match safely. Update the card before play.

The local engine automates supported card effects, legal actions, searches and choices, damage modifiers, Knockouts, Prizes, and standard turn flow. Unsupported Trainers, Tools, Abilities, and attacks fail closed without consuming cards or turn allowances. Saved card text must match a verified exact English printing before its handler is enabled.

The playmat stays local and uses browser storage. The selected-card panel and chat/log remain beside the mat on desktop. Use the **A− / A+** controls to adjust playmat, selected-card, and chat/log text sizes separately.

Drag a Basic Pokémon from hand into your Bench area to play it, including onto an empty Bench slot or elsewhere within your own Bench. Opening setup also allows a Basic from the opening hand to be dragged into the empty Active Spot; setup placement still supports Undo. During your turn, drag Energy or an Evolution card onto a highlighted eligible Pokémon. The whole card stack, including an exposed Tool tab, targets that Pokémon. Drop a Trainer onto your **Last played** area to use it; its normal costs, coin flips, searches, and cancellation rules still apply. Tools can also be dropped directly onto a Pokémon with an available Tool slot. Stadiums can be dropped into the Stadium slot or your Last played area. Dragging never skips evolution timing, turn allowances, hidden-information restrictions, or exact-print checks. Clicking a hand card still only selects it for inspection.

Janine’s Secret Art uses the docked board-selection panel to choose up to two distinct Darkness Pokémon. **Finish selection** explicitly chooses fewer targets; **Cancel** before revealing the deck restores the Supporter and its turn allowance. Required board choices do not offer Finish selection. The following Energy choices use the existing deck picker and cannot be cancelled after hidden information is revealed.

The blue playmat backgrounds remain behind both players and the central battle area. Individual Bench, discard and Stadium zones use transparent backgrounds, and Active Pokémon slots have no white backing. Decks show the blue-and-gold face-down card back; deck and discard piles share card proportions and count badges, with discard artwork fitted without cropping. Hidden deck identities stay private.

For a custom background, open **Manage decks → Playmats → Upload playmat**. Optionally enter a name, then select a JPG, PNG or WebP image (up to 12 MB). The app resizes and compresses it for browser storage. In **Start a game → Playmat**, choose an uploaded design or leave **Default · blue playmat** selected. One image covers the complete field behind both players, including the central battle area; player boundaries and zone outlines remain visible. The image fills the field and may be cropped at its edges to fit the window.

Playmat images are saved on this device and included in full backups. The chosen image is also retained in an unfinished match, so resuming does not depend on that image still being in the upload library. Removing a saved playmat resets it in the start-game picker and leaves an ongoing match’s background intact. Playmat choices do not change card rules, deck records, or the coin result.

## Reporting a problem and protecting saved data

Use **Report a problem** in the bottom-right corner, including while a card choice is open. Describe what happened and what you expected, then **Download problem report** and attach the JSON file to our chat. The report captures the state when you open the form: the app version, saved library, both players' hidden cards, turn and board state, current choices, recent messages and resolution errors, and the previous action snapshot when available. It does not change the match or send anything automatically. Reports are diagnostic files; they cannot be restored as a library or resumed as a match.

Open **Settings → Backup & restore** in the top bar and select **Download full backup** to save decks, complete saved card details, artwork references, uploaded deck pictures, play results, deletion records, and the three text-size settings. An unfinished match is excluded. Keep this file somewhere safe before clearing browser data or moving the app.

To restore, choose a full backup JSON file, review its date and deck/card counts, check the replacement confirmation, then select **Restore this backup**. This replaces saved data and ends the current match. Invalid files, unsupported backup versions, and files larger than 20 MB are rejected before saving. Finish any open choice or card update first. Restored cards still pass through the existing exact-print and complete-effect checks; saved status labels do not grant support.

Before replacement, the app saves one recovery copy in browser storage. **Download data from before the last restore** downloads that copy; choose the downloaded file to restore it through the same preview and confirmation. Each restore replaces the previous recovery copy. If there is not enough room to reserve recovery data, restoration stops before changing the library. A failed write attempts to restore the previous saved data, and the recovery download remains available if browser storage also prevents rollback.

The selected-card panel now keeps unavailable attacks and printed Abilities visible and explains their requirements beside the controls. Messages cover Energy costs, first-turn restrictions, Supporter and retreat allowances, Special Conditions, missing targets, discard choices, passive Abilities, and unsupported exact card details. The existing action checks and shared resolution transactions still enforce legality.

## Playmat effects, targeting, and match history

The playmat now has a shared center with **Codex Active — Stadium — Your Active**, with each Bench in its own player area. The discard pile shows its actual top card and count; click it to open the existing discard viewer. Prize areas show six face-down positions, with empty positions marking Prizes already taken. Prize names and artwork stay hidden.

When an effect targets a Pokémon already in play, click a highlighted Pokémon directly on the playmat. This includes Tools, switching Abilities, opposing Bench targets, attack targets, and Energy destinations. **Energy Switch** asks for its source Pokémon on the mat, then an attached Basic Energy card, then a different Pokémon on the mat. Hand, deck, discard, and attached-card choices still use the card picker. An ineligible click leaves the choice open; use **Cancel** or Escape to cancel. Existing legality checks and transactions revalidate the choice before applying it.

**Effects on/off** controls decorative effects for the current session. Coin animations display the engine's existing result before dependent choices or outcomes continue. Attack colors and particle shapes follow the attacking Pokémon's primary type, with damage indicators, a departing card for Knockouts, and victory confetti when you win. Motion preferences are respected: reduced motion shows brief static results instead of particle effects. Coin reveals briefly hold further actions while the result is displayed; Knockout and promotion presentations complete before further player or AI moves. Visuals do not change game randomness or outcomes. Canceled and rolled-back actions discard their pending decorations. New matches, undo, restore, leaving the match, and hiding the browser tab clear old effects.

At match end, **Game summary** appears beside the move log. **View full summary** shows both players' Pokémon cards played, Evolutions, physical Energy card attachments, attacks, and Knockouts. It lists the Pokémon and Energy names and printings, plus Knockout turn numbers and the latest 300 public move events. Opening Pokémon and Evolution cards count as Pokémon played. Reattaching an Energy counts as another attachment; moving attached Energy and carrying it through an Evolution do not. Double Dragon Energy counts as one physical card. Between-turn Knockouts are labeled against the turn that just ended.

In **Manage decks**, each deck's **Match history** keeps its latest 20 completed games with its win/loss perspective and full summaries. Detailed history starts with games played after this update; previous aggregate results are preserved. History is saved with existing deck statistics and included in full backups. Match records contain public play history and counts, without remaining hidden hands, decks, or Prizes. A storage failure leaves the current summary reviewable and shows that it could not be saved.

`tests/playmat-visuals-targets.test.cjs` checks real coin and attack outcomes, animation rollback and cleanup, reduced motion, direct board targeting, and visible card zones. `tests/match-history.test.cjs` checks counts, final-Prize attacks with post-damage effects, failed-win rollback, between-turn Knockouts, per-deck persistence, history limits, and backup round trips.

## Closer battle area, private setup, and played-card movement

Each player's **Last played** card sits on the outside of their Active, with its name, action, recipient when applicable, and turn. Click it to inspect the exact card text. Pausing the AI does not unlock the opponent's Control button during setup, and the copied game state masks its setup Pokémon, hand and per-card deck breakdown. Your own opening cards remain visible.
